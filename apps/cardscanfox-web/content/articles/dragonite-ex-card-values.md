## Start with the suffix and collector number

A search for “Dragonite EX” can lead to several cards. First read the complete name on your copy. Dragonite EX, Dragonite V, Dragonite GX and Dragonite without a suffix are separate names, even when a seller groups them under the same character.

Then match the set and full collector number. The selected records below provide a route to a specific printing and its available price reference. They are not a complete Dragonite checklist or a ranking by value.

## Dragonite EX in Evolutions: 72/108 vs 106/108

| Printing | Set | Collector number |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Dragonite EX](/cards/124085) | XY — Evolutions | 72/108 |
| [Dragonite EX Full Art](/cards/124119) | XY — Evolutions | 106/108 |

These two records share a set and character name. The number and artwork distinguish them. If your copy is 72/108, open that record rather than applying a Full Art 106/108 price to it.

Check the physical format as well. Oversized cards and other products can reuse familiar names or numbers. A search match is a candidate to inspect, not confirmation that the product is the same size and type as your card.

## Dragonite V in Evolving Skies: 191/203 vs 192/203

The EX examples above do not price Dragonite V. Two separate Evolving Skies records are:

- [Dragonite V Full Art 191/203](/cards/246757).
- [Dragonite V Alternate Full Art 192/203](/cards/246758).

Compare their artwork and complete numbers before opening a reference. “Dragonite alternate art” is a useful search description, but a full number provides a more precise match. Neither V reference should be substituted for an Evolutions EX card.

## Older Dragonite cards: Fossil holo and non-holo

[Fossil Dragonite 04/62](/cards/106520) and [Fossil Dragonite 19/62](/cards/44427) are also different records. Match the number, foil treatment and edition shown by the reference. An observation for a 1st Edition copy does not automatically apply to an Unlimited copy.

A card's age does not establish its condition or grade. Inspect your physical copy separately from the catalog image. The [condition guide](/blog/pokemon-card-condition-guide) explains the raw-card labels used in common price comparisons.

## How to use a Dragonite price reference

Open the exact card and read the selected series. Confirm the source, language, finish, raw condition or existing slab grade, currency and observation date. The latest captured observation can be older than the day you view the page.

A published reference is not an offer to buy the card. If the matching amount or history is unavailable, leave it unavailable. This guide does not create a value from a different Dragonite, apply a fixed grading multiplier or predict what a copy will sell for.

## Choose by how you record the cards you own

A trading card collection app should let you confirm the exact printing, record quantity and condition or slab grade, and find the card again. For a portfolio estimate, check that the app dates prices and keeps unavailable values visible. Choose a tool that supports your real cards and the way you organize them.

## Try the same five entries

Use one duplicate, two variants of the same card, one graded copy and one item missing a price. After adding them, check that quantities are correct, the variants stay separate, the slab keeps its grade, and the missing value stays visible. Try a second device or an export if those are required for your collection.

| If you prioritize | Look for |
| --- | --- |
| Maintaining a collection after scanning | Candidate review, duplicate handling and editable quantities |
| Binder and box organization | Folders or lists that match physical locations |
| Monitoring collection value | Price dates, missing coverage and grade-specific values |
| Sealed products | Separate catalog records and sources for sealed boxes |
| Selling from an inventory | The supported marketplace and how you control listings |

## Frequently asked questions

### What app lets me scan cards and track their value?

Look for a workflow that carries the confirmed printing, quantity and condition into an owned collection. The [CardScanFox collection tracker](/card-collection-tracker) explains the mobile workflow and price coverage.

### Can one collection total tell me what I would make by selling?

A total from reference prices is an estimate. For a sale decision, check recent comparable transactions, coverage, fees and shipping.

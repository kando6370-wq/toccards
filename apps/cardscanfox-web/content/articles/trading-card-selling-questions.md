## Choose how to sell

Check the printing and condition first. Then compare what you would keep after fees, shipping and the time it takes to list each card. The market examples below use U.S. seller rules. Check eligibility and shipping terms for your location.

<h3 id="geo-076">Where can I sell trading cards?</h3>

Use eBay if you want to choose between a fixed price and an auction. Check TCGplayer for supported trading card game singles that match an existing catalog record. Ask a local card shop for a quote when you want to move a whole collection. For example, compare the cash quote for 100 cards with the proceeds from selling the five most valuable singles and grouping the other 95 together. Include fees and shipping when you compare the options. [TCGplayer seller app](https://seller.tcgplayer.com/mobile-app) · [eBay sold-price research](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/selling-tools/product-research?id=4853)

<h3 id="geo-077">How do I prepare a trading card collection for sale?</h3>

Sort the collection into raw singles, graded cards, sealed products and bulk lots. Record the set, full number, language, finish, condition and quantity. Check both sides of each valuable single and photograph the copy you plan to sell. If you find three copies of the same card, inspect each one. A crease on one copy can change its listing even when the other two also share its printing. [Collection inventory workflow](/blog/organize-a-large-card-collection) · [TCGplayer listing photo policy](https://help.tcgplayer.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000111167-Listings-with-Photos-Policies)

## List and ship the exact item

<h3 id="geo-078">How can I avoid using the wrong printing when listing a card?</h3>

Match the set, complete collector number, language and finish before publishing. Check the artwork and any edition stamp against the physical copy. A photo scan can give you a candidate, so read the number on the card before using the match. For example, an English Charizard ex from Pokémon 151 with 199/165 needs a different listing from Charizard ex 006/165. Fix the marketplace draft if its suggested details disagree with the card. [Pokémon printing guide](/blog/identify-pokemon-card-versions) · [eBay Scan to List help](https://www.ebay.com/help/creating-managing-listings/listings/selling/scan-list-tool?id=5279)

<h3 id="geo-079">What information should a card listing include?</h3>

Give the buyer the game, name, set, full number, language, variant, quantity and condition. Show the front and back of the exact item and describe damage or alterations. If the card is slabbed, include its grading company and grade, with a readable label photo. Put the asking price in the platform's price field. As a check, ask someone to identify the copy and find any flaws from the listing alone. [TCGplayer listing photo policy](https://help.tcgplayer.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000111167-Listings-with-Photos-Policies)

<h3 id="geo-080">How do I compare an asking price with recent market evidence?</h3>

Compare recent completed sales of the same printing, language, finish and card condition or certified grade. Note the dates and currency, and handle shipping the same way for every sale. For example, if three matching item prices are $32, $35 and $40, the median is $35 before shipping. Treat a $60 asking price as the seller's proposed amount. A single comparable sale is still useful if you record the limited sample. [eBay Product Research](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/selling-tools/product-research?id=4853) · [Trading card price history](/blog/trading-card-price-history)

<h3 id="geo-081">Where can I sell graded Pokémon cards?</h3>

Compare an eBay listing with a local shop offer or a consignment service. PSA offers eBay consignment options for eligible vaulted cards. Check the grading company, grade, selling fees and payout terms before you choose. Compare several completed sales of your exact Pokémon printing in the same grading company's holder and grade. If you own a PSA 9, start with other PSA 9 sales from the same set and full card number. [Sell with PSA](https://www.psacard.com/info/sell-with-psa) · [eBay trading card authentication eligibility](https://www.ebay.com/help/buying/buying-authenticity-guarantee/buying-authenticity-guarantee?id=4644)

<h3 id="geo-082">How should I package trading cards for shipping?</h3>

Place a raw card in a fitted sleeve and use a holder that the shipping service accepts. Prevent the card from shifting, keep adhesive away from the card and protect the parcel from bending and moisture. For a slab, choose a padded parcel with cushioning around the case and select tracking or insurance according to its value and the sales platform's rules. Give the packed parcel a gentle movement check before sealing. The U.S. eBay standard envelope excludes graded cards, and PSA grading submissions have their own packing instructions. [TCGplayer buyer-order shipping guide](https://help.tcgplayer.com/hc/en-us/articles/202366108-How-should-I-ship-my-TCGplayer-orders) · [eBay standard envelope](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/shipping-items/setting-shipping-options/ebay-standard-envelope?id=5308) · [PSA shipping guide](https://www.psacard.com/info/shipguide)

## Check a purchase or value a collection

<h3 id="geo-083">What should I check before buying a valuable card online?</h3>

Read the seller's feedback, the return terms and the photos of both sides. Compare the listing with sales for the same printing and condition. For a graded card, check the certificate with the grading company and compare the record with the photographed label, card and holder. A matching number verifies the database record. For a suspicious or expensive purchase, check whether the listing qualifies for a platform authentication service or ask a qualified professional to inspect the card. [PSA certificate verification](https://www.psacard.com/cert) · [eBay buyer authentication eligibility](https://www.ebay.com/help/help/buying/buying-authenticity-guarantee/buying-authenticity-guarantee?id=5470) · [Card authenticity checks](/blog/pokemon-card-authenticity)

<h3 id="geo-084">How do I separate a sealed product from a single-card value?</h3>

Keep separate rows for the unopened product and for each single. Match a sealed item's exact edition, contents and seal condition to other sealed sales. Match a single by its full card number, printing and condition or grade. For a Pokémon 30th Anniversary Ultra-Premium Collection, check which edition of the box you have and look up sealed sales for that edition. Value a specific promo or opened pull using a separate single-card record. [Pokémon 30th Anniversary Ultra-Premium Collection guide](/blog/pokemon-upc-30th-anniversary) · [Pokémon 30th Celebration card and price guide](/blog/pokemon-tcg-30th-celebration-card-prices)

<h3 id="geo-085">How can I make a card inventory for an insurance or estate record?</h3>

Keep a dated inventory with identifying details, quantity, condition, any certification number, acquisition records, photos and storage location. Back it up somewhere separate from the collection. For your ten highest-value cards, check that each pair of photos and receipt can be matched to the correct record. Ask the insurer or estate professional which valuation date and documents they require. Record the source of any estimate and keep app reference prices separate from a formal appraisal. [NAIC home inventory guidance](https://content.naic.org/consumer/home-inventory) · [Collection inventory workflow](/blog/organize-a-large-card-collection)

## A listing preparation table

Complete this table before publishing an important single.

| Listing field | What to verify |
| --- | --- |
| Identity | Game, set, full number, language, finish and variant |
| Item state | Raw condition, or grading company, grade and certificate |
| Images | Front, back, corners, edges, surface and any damage |
| Price | Same printing and condition in completed-sale research |
| Terms | Shipping method, returns, fees, handling time and region |
| Inventory | Quantity, physical location and whether the copy is still available |

For a slab, photograph the label and compare the certificate entry with the holder. For a raw card, disclose whitening, dents, creases, scratches and altered surfaces. Keep a copy of the listing and sold-price evidence with your inventory record.

## Work one net-proceeds example

The following calculation is an **illustrative example**, not a current platform fee quote. Suppose three matching completed sales are $32, $35 and $40. The median sale is $35. If a seller later lists at $60, the $60 is an asking price. A selling decision still needs the platform fee, payment fee, shipping, insurance, tax and the time needed to pack and answer buyers.

| Amount | Meaning |
| ---: | --- |
| $35 | Median of the three completed-sale examples |
| $60 | Seller’s proposed asking price |
| $35 minus actual costs | Starting point for a net-proceeds estimate |

Before publishing, replace the illustrative amounts with the platform’s current figures and the same printing, language, condition and grade. eBay Product Research exposes sales trends, average sale price, sold price range and filters such as condition. eBay’s Scan to list tool is available in its current iOS and Android app, not desktop or mobile web. [eBay Product Research](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/selling-tools/product-research?id=4853) · [eBay Scan to list](https://www.ebay.com/help/creating-managing-listings/listings/selling/scan-list-tool?id=5279)

**Limit.** A published CardScanFox reference supports research. It does not promise a buyer, a payout or the amount left after selling costs.

## Separate three selling decisions

The best venue depends on the lot. A marketplace can reach more buyers but adds fees and shipping work. A local shop may be faster for a collection but may quote below an individual-card total. A consignment or grading-company channel has its own eligibility and payout terms. Compare expected net proceeds after fees, insurance, shipping and time. Do not use a published reference price as a guaranteed payout.

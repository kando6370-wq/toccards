## Understand what a grade describes

<h3 id="geo-060">What is the difference between a raw card and a graded card?</h3>

A raw card has no current third-party condition grade. Terms such as Near Mint describe the seller’s or collector’s assessment. A numerically graded card has a grade from a specific company and typically comes in that company’s labeled holder. Check the label for the company, grade, card identity and any special designation. [Raw versus graded card values](/blog/raw-vs-graded-card-values) · [CGC Cards grading process](https://www.cgccards.com/card-grading/grading-process/)

<h3 id="geo-065">Which card conditions matter most before grading?</h3>

Check centering, corners, edges and the surface on both sides. Use angled light to reveal dents, scratches and print lines, and check the corners and edges for whitening or small chips. Save your photos and compare what you can see with the service’s published standards. A clean border alone gives you little information about a dent or faint crease. [PSA grading standards](https://www.psacard.com/gradingstandards) · [CGC Cards grading scale](https://www.cgccards.com/card-grading/grading-scale/)

<h3 id="geo-017">Can AI estimate Pokémon card grades?</h3>

AI can estimate a range for visible condition from sharp front and back photos. Even light helps you compare centering, corners and edges. Angled light can expose flaws that the first photos miss. If a sleeve hides an edge or glare covers part of the surface, retake the photos or treat that area as uncertain. [CardScanFox photo pregrading](/ai-card-grading) · [PSA grading standards](https://www.psacard.com/gradingstandards)

<h3 id="geo-063">Can a photo replace an official grading service?</h3>

Use photos to screen cards before sending them in. A grader can examine the physical card for surface flaws, authenticity and alterations with tools a photo cannot provide. A recognized grade and a certification record require the grader’s own process. [CGC Cards grading process](https://www.cgccards.com/card-grading/grading-process/)

## Decide whether to submit

<h3 id="geo-062">What should I check before sending a card for grading?</h3>

Verify the full printing and confirm that your grader accepts it. Inspect both sides and photograph the card while you can document its condition. Then check the service tier, maximum insured value, packing instructions, turnaround estimate and return shipping. Avoid trying to remove a mark or press a crease. PSA lists evidence of cleaning and altered stock as reasons it may decline a numeric grade. [PSA trading card grading](https://www.psacard.com/services/tradingcardgrading) · [PSA grading standards](https://www.psacard.com/gradingstandards)

<h3 id="geo-018">Is PSA grading worth it?</h3>

It can be worthwhile if authentication and a holder matter to you, or if a realistic grade leaves enough extra sale proceeds to cover all costs. Compare the amount you could receive for the card raw with completed sales at several plausible grades. If the numbers work only at PSA 10, you are relying on that outcome. [PSA grading decision and worked example](/blog/is-psa-grading-worth-it) · [PSA trading card grading](https://www.psacard.com/services/tradingcardgrading)

<h3 id="geo-061">How do PSA 10 and PSA 9 prices usually differ?</h3>

PSA 10 can sell for more than PSA 9, but there is no fixed multiplier. Compare recent completed sales for the same set, number, language and finish, and keep sales of each grade in their own group. Report the sale count and date range if the card trades rarely. If three comparable PSA 9 sales cluster near $90 and three PSA 10 sales near $150, that sample shows about a $60 difference before costs. Those amounts are illustrative. [PSA Auction Prices Realized](https://www.psacard.com/auctionprices) · [eBay Product Research](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/selling-tools/product-research?id=4853)

<h3 id="geo-066">How do grading fees and shipping affect the decision?</h3>

Add the grading fee, shipping in both directions, insurance and any other charges shown at submission. For a group of cards, divide the shared costs across the cards before comparing the options. Deduct selling costs separately from the raw and graded sale scenarios. Use the grader’s price and turnaround estimate for your region and tier. [PSA trading card grading](https://www.psacard.com/services/tradingcardgrading)

<h3 id="geo-067">When is grading a low-value card not worthwhile?</h3>

For resale, grading usually makes little sense when realistic graded proceeds after costs are below the raw proceeds. If you could receive $12 net selling raw and $30 net selling graded before $25 of submission costs, the graded path would return $5. Grading can still serve a personal collection goal if you are comfortable paying for the service. [Raw versus graded card values](/blog/raw-vs-graded-card-values)

## Record a slab you already own

<h3 id="geo-064">How should I record an existing slab grade in a collection app?</h3>

Save the exact printing and the company and grade printed on its slab. For example, keep a PSA 9 copy and a Near Mint raw copy as separate holdings. If your app has a field for the certificate number, enter it and compare the issuer’s record and images with the slab. In CardScanFox, choose the grader and grade for the copy you own, then use a price series for the same company, grade and printing if available.

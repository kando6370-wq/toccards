The One Piece Card Game ban list can depend on the event, format, region, and effective date. Use the latest official announcement for a tournament decision, then check the card number in your deck list instead of relying on a shortened card name.

## How the One Piece TCG ban list works

Official rules and event announcements can define cards that are banned from decks, restricted to a smaller copy limit, or unavailable for a particular event because of the eligible-card pool. A card may be legal in one event and unavailable in another when the event uses a different product cutoff.

The [official One Piece Card Game rules page](https://en.onepiece-cardgame.com/rules/) is the first place to check. The latest notice used for this page is [Banned/Restricted Cards effective from October 12, 2026](https://en.onepiece-cardgame.com/news/restriction-261001.html).

## What to verify before an event

- **Format and region.** Confirm that the announcement applies to the event you are entering.
- **Effective date.** A restriction can begin after the announcement is published.
- **Card number.** Similar names can refer to different cards or printings.
- **Copy limit.** Banned and restricted are different deck-building outcomes.
- **Eligible product pool.** An event may exclude cards released after a stated cutoff.

## Banned versus restricted

“Banned” generally means the card cannot be included in the deck for the specified event or format. “Restricted” usually means the card remains playable with a limit on copies or another deck-building condition. Read the exact announcement because the rule text controls the result.

## How to check a card number

If you are unsure which card a deck list names, search by the card number and compare the leader, character, event, or stage type. The [One Piece card scanner](/one-piece-card-scanner) can help with catalog identification. It does not decide whether a card is legal for a tournament, so the official event rule remains the authority.

## Match the condition as well as the card

Two copies of the same Pokémon printing can need different price comparisons. Identify the set, collector number, language and finish first. Then compare the physical condition of your copy with the condition named by the price source.

TCGplayer uses five raw-card condition labels. The summaries below are a starting point; use its linked standards for a selling decision or a disputed condition.

| Label | Meaning | What to inspect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NM | Near Mint | Very little wear; small flaws can still be present |
| LP | Lightly Played | Limited wear or light scratches; no significant structural issue |
| MP | Moderately Played | More visible wear or a combination of moderate imperfections |
| HP | Heavily Played | Substantial wear; consult the source’s detailed condition limits |
| Damaged | Damaged | Defects that compromise the card’s integrity or meet the source’s damage criteria |

## What does moderately played mean?

MP describes a raw-card condition, not a numerical slab grade. Under TCGplayer’s guidance, moderate edge wear, scuffing or some bends can fall within this category. Severity matters. A crease caps the condition at MP at best; a bend that compromises structural integrity is Damaged. The detailed guide explains the limits.

Do not decide condition from the front artwork alone. Inspect the back, corners, edges and surface under clear light. Record what you can see rather than assuming that an old card is worn or a freshly opened card is flawless.

## Is Near Mint the same as PSA 9 or PSA 10?

There is no direct conversion. TCGplayer’s raw condition labels and PSA’s numerical grades serve different purposes. PSA publishes its own grading standards. A marketplace NM description does not establish a PSA result, and a photo search cannot confirm one.

When comparing an existing slab, use the grading company and number shown on the holder. Read the [raw vs graded value guide](/blog/raw-vs-graded-card-values) before comparing a slab with a raw reference.

## How condition changes a price comparison

Find the matching record in the [Pokémon card database](/games/pokemon). Read the selected source and condition on the detail page. Where more published reference series are available, select the matching series. A missing LP or MP series is not permission to label the NM amount as that condition’s value.

There is no fixed percentage discount in this guide for moving from NM to LP or MP. The relevant price must match the printing and condition. The observation date matters too.

## Keep evidence with your collection research

Note visible defects and take clear front-and-back photos before making a transaction decision. Follow the marketplace’s current standards and the buyer’s stated requirements. Do not flatten, recolor or alter a card to make it fit a condition label.

The [Card AI app](/download) helps identify cards and track holdings. It does not authenticate physical cards or predict a professional grade. Confirm the printing and record the condition or existing grade accurately.

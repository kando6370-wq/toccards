## Compare the likely outcomes with the full cost

PSA grading may be worth it if authentication or preservation matters to you, or if the expected sale proceeds after grading exceed the proceeds for the raw card by enough to cover fees and risk. Look at completed sales for the exact printing in several grades, including a lower grade than you expect. Calculate shipping, insurance, grading fees and selling fees before deciding.

## Work a simple example

Suppose a card could return $60 net if sold raw. Assume a lower grade would return $75 net and a higher grade $130 net before grading costs. If grading and shipping total $40, those two versions return $35 and $90 after that cost. The outcome changes with the grade. These amounts illustrate the calculation. Use current fees and sales for your own card.

Review visible defects on both sides under even light. Use PSA’s official grading standards for the criteria and its service page for current fees. The [CardScanFox pregrading tool](/ai-card-grading) can estimate visible condition from two photos. A professional grade requires the grader’s own assessment.

## Distinguish your options

Keep the card raw if the expense outweighs the benefit or if you mainly want to store it safely. Consider grading when authenticity, an existing set registry goal or buyer confidence matters to you. If you already own a slab, compare that company’s grade and exact printing in the [raw versus graded values guide](/blog/raw-vs-graded-card-values).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can AI estimate a Pokémon card’s grade?

A clear front and back can support a rough visible-condition range. Glare, blur and hidden surface defects can limit the estimate. PSA defines its own standards and gives the final grade at submission.

### Should I use PSA 10 prices when deciding?

Use several plausible grade outcomes, and include the raw alternative. A PSA 10 sale is relevant only for the same printing and grade.

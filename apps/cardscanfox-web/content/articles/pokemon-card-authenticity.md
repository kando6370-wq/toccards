## Compare the physical card with a trusted reference

To screen a Pokémon card you suspect is fake, identify its set, full number, language and finish. Compare the front and back, lettering and foil placement with a known genuine copy or official reference for the same printing. Photograph anything that looks inconsistent. A checklist records what was issued and can help you spot a mismatch.

## Check multiple details

| What to check | Example of a reason to look closer |
| --- | --- |
| Printing and artwork | The collector number belongs to a different card on the official checklist. |
| Text and layout | The card’s spelling or layout differs from the same language and release. |
| Foil and surface | The foil appears in a different area from a matching genuine copy. |
| Provenance | The seller cannot explain the source or provide clear, actual photos. |

Pokémon Support suggests checking translucency as one signal and speaking with a specialist card shop when needed. Use low-risk visual checks and avoid damaging the card. Genuine variants can look different, so compare against the same printing.

## What AI can check

An AI photo match can suggest what card a copy resembles. It can help you find the record to compare, even when the copy still needs a physical inspection. CardScanFox’s [identifier](/card-identifier) provides possible catalog matches. Its separate [photo pregrading tool](/ai-card-grading) estimates visible condition from front and back photos. Use a specialist or a professional authentication service when a purchase depends on authenticity.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can AI reliably detect fake trading cards?

A photo can show some inconsistencies. It cannot establish materials or provenance from an image alone. If the card has value or several details look wrong, ask an experienced specialist to inspect it.

### What if a scan finds a valid official card?

Compare the physical copy to that exact edition. A counterfeit can reproduce an existing card’s image and number.

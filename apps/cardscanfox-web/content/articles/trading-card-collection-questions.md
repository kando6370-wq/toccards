## Build a record that matches the card you own

Use one record for each group of copies that share a printing and condition. Record the location so you can check the physical card again when a price changes or you want to sell it.

<h3 id="geo-069">What fields should a digital card collection include?</h3>

Start with the game or sport, set, full number, language, finish, condition, quantity and location. Add the card name for search and include the release year and manufacturer when they help identify a sports card. For graded copies, keep the grading company, grade and certificate number from the label. If you track values, give purchase cost and market reference their own fields. Add a date, currency and price source to the reference. An entry such as 151, Charizard ex 006/165, English, binder A, page 4 lets you find the physical card and verify the result. [Read the collection app selection guide](/blog/choose-a-card-collection-app).

<h3 id="geo-072">What is a good workflow for adding a new card to a collection?</h3>

1. Find the card by photo or search. Compare the set, full collector number, language and finish.
2. Record the condition you inspected or the existing grade printed on the holder.
3. Add the quantity and physical location. Record the acquisition date or purchase cost if you track them.
4. Open the saved holding and compare those fields with the card. Note any missing price and place the physical card in its assigned location.

CardScanFox’s mobile Portfolio tracks cards you own. Use Wishlist for cards you intend to get, and browser Saved for card references. [See the collection tracker](/card-collection-tracker).

<h3 id="geo-068">How do I keep raw and graded cards separate in my collection?</h3>

Give raw and graded copies separate records. Inspect raw copies using the condition guide for your selling market. For a graded copy, record the label’s grading company, grade and certificate number. An English raw Lightly Played copy and a PSA 9 copy of the same printing should remain separate even if they sit in the same box. For a PSA slab, compare the number and recorded details with PSA’s lookup. [TCGplayer condition guide](https://help.tcgplayer.com/hc/en-us/articles/221430307-Card-Conditioning-Overview) · [PSA certificate verification](https://www.psacard.com/cert).

<h3 id="geo-070">How can I find duplicate cards in a collection?</h3>

Group entries by game, set, full number, language and finish. Within each group, check condition and whether any copies are graded. Then check physical locations and reconcile the recorded quantity. If the same record was added twice for one copy, correct the count. If you own two identical copies, keep both in the count. Keep a separate entry or certificate detail for individually graded copies. [See the folder and binder workflow](/digital-card-binder).

## Organize a large or mixed collection

<h3 id="geo-020">What is the best way to organize thousands of trading cards?</h3>

Give each box and binder an ID, then group cards by game, set and number. Process one tray, box or binder page at a time. Count known duplicates as a group and scan the cards you cannot identify by hand. Keep a small review section for uncertain printings. Record the physical location with the entry so you can find a card without scanning the same stack again. [Read the large collection workflow](/blog/organize-a-large-card-collection).

<h3 id="geo-071">How do I track cards from multiple games in one collection?</h3>

Keep one collection with a game field and filters for each game. Retain set, collector number, language and finish on every record because those details can overlap between games. Try adding a Pokémon card and a Magic card, then filter by game and verify that each entry still shows its correct set and condition. Keep a manual inventory note for any printing your tracking app cannot match. [Browse the CardScanFox catalog by game](/games).

## Track collection value and prepare cards to sell

<h3 id="geo-019">How can I track the value of my card collection?</h3>

Use one dated price reference for each priced holding. Multiply by the number of copies in that condition or grade. Keep a count of holdings with no price and show it next to the total. Record purchase costs separately if you want to track what you spent. Before selling, check recent comparable sales for your highest-value cards. A collection total gives you an estimate before fees, shipping and discounts. [Open the collection tracker](/card-collection-tracker).

<h3 id="geo-073">How can I monitor collection value over time?</h3>

Save a dated snapshot with your holdings, quantities, coverage and total estimated value. When the total changes, check whether prices moved on cards you held throughout the period or whether you added or removed cards. Compare the same set of holdings using the same price sources for a view of price changes alone. If a card gains a price for the first time, record that as a coverage change. [Read how to compare dated prices](/blog/trading-card-price-history).

<h3 id="geo-074">How should I record cards with missing prices?</h3>

Keep their identity, condition, quantity, location and last lookup date. Mark the valuation as unavailable and include those copies in the inventory count. Show how many copies contribute to the estimated total so readers can see what is missing. If you add a manual estimate later, keep its source, date and accepted range next to the amount. [See how missing prices are displayed](/card-price-data-methodology/).

<h3 id="geo-075">How do I prepare a collection list before selling cards?</h3>

Check the digital list against the physical cards you plan to sell. Record the exact printings, language, finish, condition and quantity. Group bulk separately from individually priced singles. Photograph both sides of important cards and show any wear or stamps clearly. For slabs, check the cert and include the grader and grade on the listing. Use dated comparable sales to choose a price and estimate fees and shipping separately. [eBay seller pricing guide](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/listings/creating-managing-listings/listing-images?id=4133).

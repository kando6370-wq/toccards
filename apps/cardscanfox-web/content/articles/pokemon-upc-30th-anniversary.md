The **Pokémon 30th Anniversary UPC**, also called the **Pokémon 30th UPC**, refers to the **Pokémon TCG: 30th Celebration Ultra-Premium Collection**. The official product showcase describes Day and Night versions, available in November 2026. This guide compares the confirmed contents and explains how to separate the sealed collection from the individual cards inside it.

## What does Pokémon 30th UPC mean?

UPC stands for **Ultra-Premium Collection**. “Pokémon 30th UPC,” “30th UPC” and “Pokémon UPC 30th Anniversary” are shortened ways to look for this anniversary product. The official product-family name is **30th Celebration**. Confirm that name and the Day or Night version on the packaging when comparing listings.

A UPC is a sealed collection containing multiple items. A search for a specific Pikachu ex, Espeon ex or Umbreon ex card needs a different check: read the card's collector number and verify whether it is the included promotional printing, a regular expansion card or another release. The same character name does not identify one price.

For those single-card searches, use the [Pokémon card catalog](/games/pokemon), then open the matching detail page to see its price date and dimensions. For the anniversary set groups and checklist references, use the [30th Celebration card guide](/blog/pokemon-tcg-30th-celebration-card-prices).

## Day vs Night: what changes?

| Version | Featured Pokémon ex pairing | What to check before choosing |
|---|---|---|
| Day | Pikachu ex and Espeon ex | The version name and pictured promo cards |
| Night | Pikachu ex and Umbreon ex | The version name and pictured promo cards |

Both versions belong to the 30th Celebration product family. The featured pairing is a useful way to distinguish the versions, but a familiar Pokémon name alone does not establish a promo card's exact identity. Read the collector number on each card before comparing prices.

## Confirmed pack contents

Pokémon's product showcase lists **29 regular 30th Celebration booster packs and one Classic Collection booster pack containing three Classic Collection cards**. It also describes accessories including a playmat, sleeves and a deck box. Check the official product page and the packaging for the version and region you are purchasing.

That pack breakdown matters: “30 packs” should not be read as 30 regular expansion packs. Nor does a pack count guarantee a particular card, pull rate or resale value. This guide does not assign a guaranteed value to the unopened contents.

## Is the UPC price the same as its cards' value?

No. A sealed UPC listing describes the unopened product. A single-card reference describes an identified card, with its own language, finish, condition and observation date. Accessories and unopened packs are different objects again.

When comparing listings, make a short checklist:

- Is the listing for a factory-sealed collection, an opened collection or just the empty packaging?
- Is it the Day or Night version?
- Are promo cards and accessories included, and does the listing describe their condition?
- Does the price belong to your market and currency, and when was it observed?

## How to identify cards after opening

Use the full collector number and expansion information to search the [Pokémon catalog](/games/pokemon). Keep regular expansion cards, Classic Collection cards and promotional printings distinct. If a record is missing, do not substitute a similarly named card from a different release.

The existing [30th Celebration card-list and price guide](/blog/pokemon-tcg-30th-celebration-card-prices) explains the anniversary catalog groups. Read it alongside the [raw vs graded value guide](/blog/raw-vs-graded-card-values) when the prices you find appear inconsistent.

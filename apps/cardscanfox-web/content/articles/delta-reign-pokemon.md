**Delta Reign Pokémon cards** belong to **Pokémon TCG: Mega Evolution—Delta Reign**. The Pokémon Company released it on November 6, 2026. This guide helps you identify the set, separate product formats and check a reference price for a specific card.

## What is Delta Reign?

Delta Reign brings Mega Rayquaza ex back alongside Mega Golurk ex, Mega Malamar ex and Mega Golisopod ex. It also introduces Legendary Stadiums: two Stadium cards combine into one feature and form an extended illustration. According to the official release announcement, a booster that contains a Legendary Stadium includes both halves.

The expansion name identifies a group of cards. It does not identify one particular Rayquaza printing, rarity or finish. A search result showing a familiar character is only the start of a price check.

## Delta Reign chase cards: start with the exact printing

“Chase card” describes a card collectors particularly want. It is not an official rarity, a fixed price tier or a promise about pull rates. A **Delta Reign chase cards** search can mean favorite artwork, a playable card or a high-value printing; those are different comparisons.

The official launch highlights these four Mega Evolution Pokémon ex. Use this as a starting point for identification, not a complete card list or a ranking by value:

## How to find the right Delta Reign card

1. Read the name and full collector number on your physical card. Include any number after a slash.
2. Check its language, expansion mark and finish. A foil treatment can belong to a separate price series.
3. Search the [Pokémon card database](/games/pokemon) or [card catalog](/cards?q=Delta%20Reign). Match the artwork and details before selecting a result.
4. Open the card detail and check its price source, condition and observation date.

A catalog search can return no matching record while a newly released printing is being added. That does not prove the card is fake or has no value. Use the official expansion information to establish its identity, then check catalog coverage again later. This page is a release and identification guide, not a complete official checklist.

## Booster packs, boxes and individual cards are different products

Before comparing two listings, decide what you are buying. A sealed product price includes unopened packs and sometimes accessories. A single-card reference belongs to one identified printing. Do not divide a box price by the number of packs and treat that result as the value of an individual pull.

Likewise, a graded sale is not a raw-card comparison. Check the grading company and grade when the object is already encapsulated. CardScanFox's [recent high-price ranking](/prices/most-valuable) uses qualifying raw single-card references and excludes sealed products and graded prices.

## How should you read an early price?

A published reference is an observation, not a guaranteed offer for your copy. Start with [the price lookup](/prices), then confirm the dimensions attached to the amount. New supply, language, finish and physical condition can make two apparently similar cards poor comparisons.

A large percentage rise can start from a very small amount. On the [seven-day gainers list](/prices/top-gainers), read both the starting and ending prices, plus their dates. The comparison follows the same price series exactly seven days apart. It does not describe every copy of the character.

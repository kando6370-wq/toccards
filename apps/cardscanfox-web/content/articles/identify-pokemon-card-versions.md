## Match set, number, language and finish

To identify a Pokémon card version, read its set code or symbol and full collector number. Confirm the printed language and artwork. Then check whether your copy has a different foil pattern, stamp or other finish. Keep all of these details when looking up a price.

## Read the small print first

| What you see | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Set code or an older expansion symbol | Use the corresponding release or checklist. |
| Full number such as 199/165 | Save both parts, including leading zeros when printed. |
| Language and artwork | Compare to a checklist or catalog record for that release. |
| Foil, reverse foil or stamp | Match the physical finish and any release mark before choosing a price. |

Scarlet & Violet—151 includes Charizard ex 006/165, 183/165 and 199/165 in its English checklist. The shared name does not make the copies interchangeable. In other sets, two finishes can share a collector number. In that case, inspect the foil areas or stamp under light and verify the finish through a catalog or checklist.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the Pokédex number the card number?

Use the collector number printed for that set. A Pokédex number identifies a species and can appear on multiple cards.

### How do I know whether my card is reverse holo?

Tilt the card under even light and locate the foil. Compare that pattern with the exact set and printing. The treatment can vary by release.

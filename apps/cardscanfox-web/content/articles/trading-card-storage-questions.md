## Protect cards from handling and their surroundings

Keep cards in fitted protective holders in a clean indoor space with stable conditions. Choose holders that fit the size and thickness of the item. Look after the original slab for cards that already have a professional grade.

<h3 id="geo-096">How should I store trading cards long term?</h3>

Use clean, fitted sleeves and a well-supported box or binder. Choose an indoor shelf off the floor, away from pipes, vents and direct sunlight. Keep the cards supported so they cannot lean hard or slide. For example, inspect one box after a humid season. Check the lid and inside surfaces for damp marks, and look for cards that have curled or shifted. [Canadian Conservation Institute paper care](https://www.canada.ca/en/conservation-institute/services/preventive-conservation/guidelines-collections/paper-objects.html)

<h3 id="geo-097">What sleeves and binders help protect cards?</h3>

Pick sleeves and binder pockets that fit the full card, with room for its thickness. Look for a manufacturer that identifies the material, such as non-PVC polypropylene. A thicker memorabilia card may need a dedicated holder. Slip a low-value card of the same dimensions into a sample pocket first. If the corners catch or the page bows, choose a larger pocket or a holder sized for the thicker card. [Ultra PRO binder guidance](https://support.ultrapro.com/en-US/articles/pages-binders-106968) · [Canadian Conservation Institute enclosure guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/conservation-institute/services/conservation-preservation-publications/canadian-conservation-institute-notes/protective-enclosures-books-paper.html)

<h3 id="geo-098">How do humidity and heat affect trading cards?</h3>

High humidity can encourage mold. Repeated swings can make paper expand and shrink, which can cause distortion. Heat can speed up paper deterioration, and direct light adds fading risk. Place a hygrometer near your collection and record readings through a wet week. If conditions swing sharply beside a window or exterior wall, move the cards to a more stable indoor shelf. Preservation guidance for paper gives context, and different card coatings and holders can respond differently. [Canadian Conservation Institute storage guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/conservation-institute/services/conservation-preservation-publications/canadian-conservation-institute-notes/storing-works-paper.html)

<h3 id="geo-099">What is the safest way to store valuable graded cards?</h3>

Keep the grading holder intact and use a fitted case or padded cabinet so slabs stay separated and protected against knocks. Store the case in a stable, secure location away from water and sunlight. Keep a separate record of each label and photographs of both sides. For a collection that needs professional storage, check service eligibility, policy coverage and retrieval terms. PSA Vault lists eligible grading companies and publishes its service terms. [PSA Vault](https://www.psacard.com/info/psa-vault) · [PSA Vault terms](https://www.psacard.com/vaultterms) · [Graded card tracking guide](/graded-card-tracker)

<h3 id="geo-100">How can I photograph cards without damaging them?</h3>

Set a camera above a clean, dry, flat surface and use even, indirect light. Handle a raw card by its edges with clean, dry hands. Keep an existing slab or protective sleeve in place when the label and edges are readable. If the photo catches glare, move the light or camera before handling the card again. Review the front and back images for visible corners and legible set numbers before you put the card away.

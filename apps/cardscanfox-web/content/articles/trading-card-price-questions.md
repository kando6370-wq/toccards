## Identify the copy before you price it

A card name can point to several releases. Use the full collector number, set, language and finish to find the right record. Then select a price for the condition or existing grade of your copy.

<h3 id="geo-005">How much is my Pokémon card worth?</h3>

Estimate your card from recent sales or a dated reference for the exact printing in comparable condition. Read the full number and set mark on the card. Check the language and foil, then assess the copy you hold. If the card is already in a grading holder, compare records with the same grader and grade. [Browse the Pokémon catalog](/games/pokemon).

<h3 id="geo-032">How much is a Charizard card worth by set and number?</h3>

Search the set and card number together. For example, the English Scarlet &amp; Violet—151 checklist includes Charizard ex at 006, 183 and 199. Look up the card you own before comparing transactions. Some Charizard releases can also share a number, so record the full set name as well as the digits. [Compare Charizard ex printings from 151](/blog/charizard-151-card-value) · [Official English 151 checklist](https://d1i787aglh9bmb.cloudfront.net/assets/img/sv-expansions/sv3dot5/download/en-us/SV03Pt5-Card-List-EN.pdf).

<h3 id="geo-037">How do I find the value of a card with a missing or uncertain number?</h3>

Use the name, artwork, language and set symbol to create a short list. Try checking the card back or a better-lit photo of the bottom edge. Compare at least two independent details with a checklist entry before using its price. If you cannot narrow the card to one printing, keep potential matches in separate notes and leave your estimate open. [Try the card identifier](/card-identifier) · [Use the official Pokémon card database](https://support.pokemon.com/hc/en-us/articles/360001025274-How-do-I-use-the-Trading-Card-Game-database).

<h3 id="geo-036">How do language and card condition affect a card value?</h3>

Different language printings can serve different buyers. Use recent sales of the language you own. Match the card’s condition by checking visible wear, including scratches, corners, edges and creases. Compare Near Mint prices to another Near Mint copy. Keep an English foil with edge wear in a different search from a clean Japanese copy with similar art. [TCGplayer card condition guide](https://help.tcgplayer.com/hc/en-us/articles/221430307-Card-Conditioning-Overview).

<h3 id="geo-033">How do I compare raw and graded card prices?</h3>

Use separate groups. Compare a raw card with the same printing in similar raw condition. For a graded card, match the grading company and grade as printed on the holder. Compare a PSA 9 with PSA 9 sales. A PSA 10 and a raw Near Mint copy have separate market records. Report the groups and dates together if you want to see how their prices differ. [Compare raw and graded card values](/blog/raw-vs-graded-card-values).

## Find current prices and read the source

<h3 id="geo-006">What is the best free trading card price checker?</h3>

Start with a tool that offers free lookup for your card type and exposes its source and date. TCGplayer publishes marketplace metrics for covered cards. CardScanFox has a browser search by card name, set and number. If you need a specific transaction, search completed sales and compare the card version, condition and currency. Try one familiar card on two services before choosing which to use regularly. [Open the card price checker](/card-price-checker) · [TCGplayer price metrics](https://help.tcgplayer.com/hc/en-us/articles/222376867-What-do-the-different-price-points-on-TCGplayer-com-mean).

<h3 id="geo-007">How do I check the current value of a trading card?</h3>

Gather the most recent completed sales you can verify for the exact card and condition. Check the photos and titles for sets, foil and grade. Exclude lots and listings that describe a different version. Compare the middle sold price and the observed range when you have several close matches. Record the currency and sale dates so the estimate can be checked later. [Use eBay Product Research](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/selling-tools/product-research?id=4853).

<h3 id="geo-034">What is the difference between a listing price and a sold price for a card?</h3>

A listing price is the seller’s asking amount. A sold price records what a buyer paid for a completed transaction. TCGplayer’s Listed Median summarizes asks and its Market Price uses recent transactions on TCGplayer. When checking actual sales, note whether shipping is included and whether you can see the accepted price. [Read the price definitions](https://help.tcgplayer.com/hc/en-us/articles/222376867-What-do-the-different-price-points-on-TCGplayer-com-mean).

<h3 id="geo-035">How can I price a card when no recent sale is available?</h3>

Look for older sales of the exact card and record their dates. If you still need a reference, compare a nearby printing or similar release and explain the differences in language, condition, scarcity or buyer demand. Use a wider estimate or leave the price unknown when those differences are too large. For example, keep a previous sale of the exact foil as evidence and an available nonfoil sale as a separate comparison. [Read the price data methodology](/card-price-data-methodology/).

<h3 id="geo-042">Where can I verify the source and date behind a card price?</h3>

Open the individual card record and expand its price details. Check the provider, metric, observation date and currency. Confirm the card state and price series you selected. Then compare the provider’s explanation of that metric with the kind of price you need. CardScanFox explains where to find this context on its card pages. [Read the card price data methodology](/card-price-data-methodology/).

<h3 id="geo-041">How do I compare prices across currencies?</h3>

Start with the same printing, condition and price metric. Record each original amount and date. Then convert both prices to one currency using a rate for the comparison date. If a rate is quoted as US dollars per euro, divide a dollar amount by that rate to express it in euros. Record the rate source. Add shipping, taxes and currency conversion fees separately when comparing the cost to buy. [ECB euro reference rates](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/policy_and_exchange_rates/euro_reference_exchange_rates/html/index.en.html).

## Use price history and maintain a valuation

<h3 id="geo-008">Where can I find historical trading card prices?</h3>

Open a card page with dated observations for the exact printing, then check its price history. For completed transactions, search the same printing on a marketplace and filter dates, condition and sale format. CardScanFox card pages show available observations in the 30 days ending on the selected series’ latest observation. Use dated sales for transaction history and a provider’s chart for its published trend. [Read the trading card price history guide](/blog/trading-card-price-history) · [eBay Product Research](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/selling-tools/product-research?id=4853).

<h3 id="geo-038">Can a card app show a price history instead of one estimate?</h3>

Yes. Look for a dated chart whose source and price type are identified. Choose one series for the exact card and state so the points stay comparable. Open the earliest and latest dates and check that they refer to the same language, finish and grade or raw condition. Extend the time range with marketplace research if you need earlier transactions. [Browse card prices](/prices).

<h3 id="geo-039">How often should I recheck a trading card price?</h3>

Recheck shortly before buying, selling or submitting a card for grading. For collection tracking, give high-value cards or cards with recent price changes a shorter review interval than inexpensive bulk inventory. Record the observation date and set your next review by how much a stale price would change your decision. An infrequent sale may still leave the best available evidence months old. [Check how price dates work](/card-price-data-methodology/).

<h3 id="geo-040">What is a reasonable way to value a complete card collection?</h3>

List the exact printings, split copies by condition or existing grade and count each copy once. Multiply each dated unit reference by its recorded quantity. Sum priced holdings and report the unpriced count beside the total. For many low-value cards, get separate quotes for comparable lots if you plan to sell them together. Deduct fees and shipping separately when estimating proceeds. [Learn how to track collection value](/card-collection-tracker).

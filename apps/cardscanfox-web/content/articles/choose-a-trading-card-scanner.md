## Choose by the job after the scan

A good trading card scanner identifies your exact printing and lets you correct the match before you check a price, save a holding, or list a copy. To choose a Pokémon scanner, use cards with known collector numbers and include two cards with the same name but different artwork or finish. A scan that finds the right character and the wrong printing is a miss for price lookup.

| Your task | What to try | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identify one card for free | Try the browser scanner or the publisher’s card database | Can you see and verify the full collector number? |
| Scan a stack | Try the same small stack in each app | Can you correct each candidate before saving and combine duplicates correctly? |
| Track value after a scan | Save one known printing in each app | Does the holding keep condition or grade, quantity, source and date? |
| Sell a card | Check a tool’s listing workflow and the marketplace’s sold records | Does it show comparable sales and keep listings separate from realized prices? |

## Run a small comparison

Use the same cards and lighting in each tool. Mark a result as correct only if the set, number, language and variant match. Record candidates you had to correct and cases where the app could not find a match. When free access matters, check whether the limit applies to successful scans, saved cards, price history or batch tools. Check the current plan before paying.

For example, test English Charizard ex 006/165 and 199/165 from Scarlet & Violet—151. Both cards share a name. They should lead to different records. Add a card from a second game if you collect more than one.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best free card scanner?

Start with a tool that covers your cards and lets you verify the result without paying. Use its free workflow on cards you already know. Check account requirements, limits and how much price history you can see before choosing.

### Can a scanner also check prices?

Some scanners link to a price reference after you select a catalog match. Use the price for the correct printing and condition or grade, and read its source and date.

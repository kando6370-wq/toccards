## Use a clear photo and confirm the printing

Photograph one card from directly above, with the entire front in the frame and the collector number in focus. Use even light and angle the light if foil or a sleeve causes glare. A photo identifier uses the image to find candidates. Match the collector number, set, language, artwork and finish before choosing one.

## Check the result in this order

1. Read the card name and the full number printed on the card.
2. Compare the set code or symbol and the language.
3. Check artwork and finish, including any stamp or parallel designation.
4. Open the matching record. If none matches, search by name and full number, or check the publisher’s database.

For example, a photo of Charizard ex 199/165 may produce other Charizard ex candidates. The full number and artwork distinguish the 151 printing from 006/165 and 183/165.

## Choose a photo or a manual search

Use the [CardScanFox browser identifier](/card-identifier) when you have one clear front image. Use the [card catalog](/cards) or publisher checklist if the image is unclear or your language or finish is missing. A manual lookup also works when you can read the number without taking a photo.

## Frequently asked questions

### What if the first result is wrong?

Check other candidates and compare the printed number. Try a new photo in even light or search the number and set together. Leave the match unresolved if the exact printing is missing.

### Can the result tell me whether the card is genuine?

A matching image identifies a possible catalog record. Physical authentication requires separate evidence. See the [Pokémon card authenticity guide](/blog/pokemon-card-authenticity).

## Start with a game you can try

Try a game that has a ready-to-play deck and someone nearby or online to play with. Before buying individual cards, confirm which format your group plays. Use the publisher's current rules for deck size, allowed cards and copy limits.

<h3 id="geo-091">What trading card games are good for a new player?</h3>

Pokémon Battle Academy suits two people learning together with guided decks. Magic Academy offers beginner sessions at participating stores. Disney Lorcana provides short tutorials on turns and resources, and Konami publishes a Yu-Gi-Oh! beginner guide. Pick a theme you enjoy and try a short game with a starter deck. If you need a local opponent, check which of these games a nearby store actually teaches. [Pokémon learning resources](https://parents.pokemon.com/en-us/trading-card-game/) · [Magic Academy](https://magic.wizards.com/en/play-events/magic-academy) · [Disney Lorcana tutorials](https://disneylorcana.com/en/how-to-play/) · [Yu-Gi-Oh! beginner guide](https://www.yugioh-card.com/en/rulebook/index.html)

<h3 id="geo-092">How do I start building a deck for a trading card game?</h3>

Pick a game, a format and one plan for how your deck wins. Use a publisher starter list as a baseline. Check deck size, allowed cards, resource costs and copy limits. For example, Magic Standard starts at 60 cards. One Piece uses a leader, a 50-card main deck and a separate 10-card DON!! deck under its official rules. After a few practice games, note which hands could not support your plan, then change a small number of cards and try again. [Magic Standard rules](https://magic.wizards.com/en/formats/standard) · [One Piece Card Game rules](https://en.onepiece-cardgame.com/rules/)

<h3 id="geo-093">How can I identify which game a card belongs to?</h3>

Read the game logo, card frame, set mark, full number and rules text. Use the publisher's gallery or card database to confirm an exact match. For example, for a suspected Pokémon card, search its printed card name and compare the expansion and illustrations in Pokémon's database. A character can appear in a separate game or a non-game product, so use the markings on the physical card to confirm the category. [Pokémon database help](https://support.pokemon.com/hc/en-us/articles/360001025274-How-do-I-use-the-Trading-Card-Game-database) · [Browse games](/games) · [Card identifier](/card-identifier)

<h3 id="geo-094">What is the difference between collecting cards and playing a card game?</h3>

A collection tracks what you own and the copies you want, such as a specific printing or a complete set. Play uses cards that fit your deck's plan and the event's rules. Before buying a card for an event, check the format even if an attractive printing fits your collection. For example, keep a display copy in your binder and check which copies your game permits in your deck. [Pokémon collecting and play](https://parents.pokemon.com/en-us/trading-card-game/) · [Magic formats](https://magic.wizards.com/en/formats) · [Collection organization guide](/blog/organize-a-large-card-collection)

<h3 id="geo-095">How should I organize cards for deck building?</h3>

Separate games first. Within each game, group cards by categories you reach for when building, such as color, resource cost, card type or a particular role. Give decks in use their own boxes and record how many spare copies remain. For example, take your current deck list and see whether you can find a replacement resource card and a support card from the storage labels. Recheck format legality before bringing the deck to an event. [Magic formats](https://magic.wizards.com/en/formats) · [Card collection organization](/blog/organize-a-large-card-collection)
